| <a href="https://youtu.be/o5hu9f9P6Do?t=6360" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">1:46:00</a> | Did the Egyptians have flood myths, and how do they differ from or correspond to the biblical flood and other ancient Near Eastern flood stories? | Egypt had annual Nile flooding, creation-water motifs, and ritual box imagery instead of a direct counterpart to Mesopotamian deluge stories. | Egypt's annual Nile flood made destructive inundation a recurring experience. Hapi is a god of the Nile's abundance and its destructive floods. These traditions do not supply a Noah's-ark story; the biblical flood narrative more closely resembles Gilgamesh in that respect. *Tebba*, the word for Noah's ark, is an Egyptian loanword meaning a general-purpose box. The temple ritual offers a stronger Egyptian parallel to Noah's ark, alongside Egyptian water myths and wider Near Eastern flood traditions. |
